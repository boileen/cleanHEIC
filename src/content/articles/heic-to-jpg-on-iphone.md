# How to Convert HEIC to JPG on iPhone

iPhone photos are commonly saved as HEIC, which is efficient on the device but awkward elsewhere. You can change future capture settings or export compatible copies of photos you already have.

## Quick answer

On the iPhone, Settings > Camera > Formats can switch future captures to Most Compatible. For existing HEIC photos, export or convert a separate JPEG copy and keep the original.

## Step-by-step workflow

### Step 1: Check the iPhone capture setting

On supported devices, Settings > Camera > Formats offers High Efficiency and Most Compatible. This generally changes future capture, not existing photos.

### Step 2: Choose a route for existing photos

Export or share a compatible copy using a supported app, or transfer the original to a trusted computer and convert there.

### Step 3: Test the output extension and content

Open the new image and confirm it is JPEG rather than relying on the name shown by an app.

### Step 4: Retain the original photo

Store the original separately; a JPEG export can omit data or metadata from the source.

## How this applies to your situation

### A distinction that prevents confusion

Changing the iPhone camera format controls future captures; it does not retroactively convert every existing .heic file already in Photos or iCloud.

## Common mistakes to avoid

- Changing only the filename: The bytes inside a HEIC container do not become JPEG merely because the suffix is changed.
- Deleting the only original: A new JPEG may be resized or missing metadata. Keep the source until the output is fully checked.
- Assuming every export keeps all metadata: Location, date, orientation and colour information may be copied, removed or rewritten depending on software.
- Ignoring the final application: A technically valid JPEG still may fail a portal because of maximum size, dimensions or aspect-ratio requirements.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can I convert HEIC photos on an iPhone?

You can change the camera format for future shots or use the export options in Photos. Browser-based converters also work once the HEIC files are on the device or a computer.

### Will the image look exactly the same after conversion?

JPEG compression and colour processing can change pixels, even when the image has the same dimensions. Test an output at your intended viewing size.

### Will changing the extension from HEIC to JPEG work?

No. An extension rename does not perform image decoding and JPEG encoding.

### Can I keep the original photo?

Yes, and that is recommended. Export a new compatible copy to a different folder.

### What if the target website still rejects the file?

Confirm the file really is JPEG, then check size, dimensions, colour mode, corruption and the website’s current requirements.

## Final checklist

- Original HEIC safely retained
- Export is a genuine JPEG file
- Output opens in an independent viewer
- Dimensions, colour and orientation checked
- Metadata and privacy requirements reviewed
- Target service accepts the result
